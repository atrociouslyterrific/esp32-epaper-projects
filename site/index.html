Pip is a squishy blob: it scoots to its bed to nap, its bowl to eat and its box to hide, and stretches and squashes as it jumps. The board does the same moves at e-paper speed (about 3 frames a second); here they're tweened smoothly. How happy Pip is getting

Each point is one training update (32,768 simulated 3-minute steps). Reward is how well Pip's needs are met, plus enjoying activities that fit its personality. Higher means a happier Pip. The thick line is a 10-update average.

How well Pip is learning to talk

The voice model's loss: how surprised it is by the next letter in its example sentences. Lower is better. It drops fast at first, then levels off as Pip's sentences start making sense.

Night by night

The history starts with the next training run.

Every 2 hours GitHub continues training from the last version, then tests the new brain and voice against the old ones on the same test worlds and sentences. The new one is kept only if it does at least as well. Brain score: higher is better. Voice loss: lower is better.

Inside Pip's voice

Left: every letter (and mood code) the voice can read. Middle: its memory cells. They feed back into themselves after every letter (the loop), which is how Pip remembers what it has written so far; three "gates" decide what to keep, forget and add. Right: the letters it can write next. Lines are the real weights (only the strongest are drawn): teal pushes up, orange pushes down. Press Write a sentence to watch it work: the letter going in lights up, memory cells glow by how active they are, and each possible next letter grows with how likely it is.

This runs Pip's real voice model in your browser with the same numbers as the board. It writes one letter at a time: the bars show the letters it was choosing between for the last letter. "Wildness" is the temperature. 0 always picks the most likely letter; higher values take more chances. The board uses 0.75.
